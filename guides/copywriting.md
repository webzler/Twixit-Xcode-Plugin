# Writing the headline and supporting text

Each screenshot says one thing. The **headline** is the benefit; the **supporting text** is the proof or the detail. People skim, so write for a glance.

## Rules of thumb

- **Headline:** 2–5 words and about 30 characters at most. It's a benefit or an outcome, not a feature name: "Plan your week in minutes", not "Calendar view".
  - Start with a verb or the result.
  - Use a line break (`\n`) to balance two lines, for example "Plan your week\nin minutes".
- **Supporting:** one short sentence, about 60 characters at most, saying how or what exactly: "Drag tasks onto any day — reminders follow."
- **One idea per screenshot.** Match the text to what's visible inside the device, so the screenshot proves the claim.
- **The first three tell the story alone:**
  1. what it is, or the main benefit;
  2. the core action;
  3. what makes it different.

  Later screenshots cover depth: sync, widgets, privacy, customization.
- **Voice:** match the app. Playful apps can be playful; finance and health apps stay calm and precise. Use the app's own words for its features (from its UI strings).
- **Consistency:** keep the same grammar across the set, for example all imperative ("Track…", "Share…", "Find…") or all outcomes.
- **Avoid:**
  - prices, "free", "#1", "best", rankings and awards (unless the user provides them);
  - other companies' or apps' names;
  - claims the app can't back up;
  - all caps, exclamation marks everywhere, and emoji.

  App Review guideline 2.3 requires accurate metadata.

## Process

1. List the screens you captured. For each, write down what the user sees and why it matters to them.
2. Draft 2–3 headline options per screen and pick the clearest. Read the first three in a row: do they make someone want the app?
3. Write the supporting line for each.
4. Check the lengths. If a headline is longer than about 30 characters, cut it rather than shrinking the font.
5. **Other languages:**
   - Translate the meaning, not the words, and keep within the same lengths. German and French run 20–30% longer, so use shorter phrasing.
   - Japanese, Chinese and Korean need fewer characters but still one idea.
   - Use the app's own translations for feature names (from its String Catalog).
   - If you aren't confident in a language, say so and suggest the user has it reviewed.

## Example (a to-do app)

| # | Screen | Headline | Supporting |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Today list | Your day, sorted | Today's tasks first, the rest when you need them. |
| 2 | Quick add | Add it before you forget | Type naturally — "call Sam friday 3pm" just works. |
| 3 | Week board | See your whole week | Drag tasks between days to rebalance. |
| 4 | Widget | Glance, don't open | Today's tasks right on your Home Screen. |
| 5 | Shared list | Share lists with anyone | Groceries, trips and projects stay in sync. |
